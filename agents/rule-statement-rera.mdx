## What it does

The Rule Statement & RERA agent is a legal writing specialist. Tell it what element you're briefing and give it your client's facts — it finds the cases, pulls the court's own operative language, and drafts brief-ready RE and RA paragraphs with inline Bluebook citations.

The output is brief-ready: active voice, no hedging, Bluebook citations as hyperlinks you can click through to the opinion.

## How to use

Start by navigating to **Kiwi AI** from the left sidebar of the dashboard when you log in. Then in your prompt, mention **"rule statement"**, **"RE/RA"**, or **"RERA"** — once you send the message, the agent activates automatically.

You can also paste case names directly — the agent will use those instead of searching.

## What to include

The more context you give upfront, the stronger the output:

| What | Required | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Legal element | Yes | The specific argument being supported — e.g., "failure to warn under strict liability" |
| Client facts | Yes | The facts of your client's case — the agent will ask if missing |
| Jurisdiction | Recommended | Circuit, district, or state — defaults to Federal |
| Audience | Optional | `judge`, `jury`, or `memo` — tailors formality |
| Your theory | Optional | Your initial read on why your client wins — the agent builds on it rather than ignoring it |
| Counterarguments | Optional | Anticipated weaknesses — used to sharpen the affirmative argument |

## What you get back

1. **Case analysis** — a prose paragraph on which cases were found and what each contributes to the argument, with inline hyperlinked citations
2. **Rule Explanation (RE)** — synthesized from the top 2–3 cases using the court's own language
3. **Rule Application (RA)** — applies the rule to your client's facts, draws factual analogies, flags 1–2 weaknesses
4. **Lead case recommendation** — one sentence on which case to lead the RE with and why
5. **Follow-up actions** — 1–2 next steps you can take

<Note>
  If a rule statement comes from a concurrence rather than the majority, the agent flags it inline. If all rule statements are from majority opinions, nothing extra is said.
</Note>

## Example prompts

**Drafting a 12(b)(6) motion:**
> "Draft RE and RA for a 12(b)(6) motion to dismiss. Element: failure to plead a plausible claim for fraudulent misrepresentation. Jurisdiction: SDNY. Client facts: plaintiff alleges in conclusory terms that our client 'intentionally deceived' him but pleads no specific misstatement, no date, no speaker. Audience: judge."

**Starting from a case you already know:**
> "I want rule statements from *Iqbal* and *Twombly* for a Rule 12(b)(6) brief on pleading plausibility. Client was sued for breach of contract — complaint has no specific allegations of what term was breached or when. Draft the RE/RA."

**Going into trial on a negligence claim:**
> "Generate RERA paragraphs for the duty element in a premises liability case in the 11th Circuit. Client is a grocery store — plaintiff slipped on water near the entrance. No employee saw the spill. My theory: no constructive notice because no evidence the spill existed long enough to put the store on notice."

**Appellate brief, with your theory and known weakness:**
> "I'm writing the argument section of a 5th Circuit appellate brief. Element: whether plaintiff exhausted administrative remedies under the ADEA. My theory is exhaustion was excused because the agency's process was futile — they had a policy of denying all claims in this category. Anticipated counterargument: plaintiff never attempted to file. Draft RE and RA with that weakness flagged."

**Quick rule extraction from a single case:**
> "Pull the rule statement on willful copyright infringement from *BMG Rights Management v. Cox Enterprises*. Client facts: ISP had notice of repeated infringement by a subscriber but did not terminate. Score it against my facts and tell me if this is the right lead case."

## Tips for better output

- **Include your theory.** If you already have a read on why your client wins, say it. The agent builds on your reasoning rather than starting from scratch.
- **Name the element precisely.** "Negligence" produces weaker results than "breach of the duty to warn under strict products liability."
- **Paste cases you've already found.** The agent will use them as the starting point and supplement if needed.
- **Flag the weakness yourself.** If you know the strong counterargument, include it — the agent will harden the affirmative argument against it rather than leaving it unaddressed.
- **Name the opposition.** Add the opposing party or counsel to your prompt and the agent will profile them, add an *Opposing Counsel / Party Profile* section, and use their likely counterarguments to sharpen the Rule Application. See [Defendant Claim Analysis](/agents/defendant-claim-analysis).
